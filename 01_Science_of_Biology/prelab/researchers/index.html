This tool is your "training gym." Every click is practice that strengthens your science muscles.

🧭 Why You're Doing This

Many undergraduates do not automatically develop strong scientific‑reasoning skills with time alone. Targeted, step‑by‑step practice and authentic research experiences—like this activity—are associated with measurable gains in experimental design and scientific literacy within a single semester. This activity allows you to practice the steps one at a time, with:

  • Clear examples from real UWP researchers
  • Built-in checklists so you always know what belongs in your answer
  • Immediate feedback to guide your thinking
  • 10 different scenarios — from gut bacteria to fossil dig sites

You're not memorizing a method — you're learning to think like a scientist.

🛠 How to Use This Tool
  1. Choose a UW Parkside Researcher Card below — this is your scenario.
    Think of it like picking a mission. Each researcher found something surprising in the real world.
  2. Complete the 8 steps.
    Each page presents one step. Write your answer, check the feedback, adjust if needed, then move on.
  3. Use the checklists every time.
    The built-in keyword system will tell you if you've hit the required concepts. It won't let you skip.
  4. When you finish all 8 steps: email your work.
    Only the current researcher's answers are sent.
  5. Try another researcher any time.
    Every researcher gives you a new puzzle. The more you try, the faster you master the process.
Friendly Tips
✔ One Step at a TimeDon't think about all 8 steps at once. Each screen only asks for one thing.
✔ Don't Try to Be PerfectWrite something → check feedback → adjust. That's exactly how real scientific thinking works.
✔ Take BreaksDo 2–3 steps, stand up, move around, reset. Small bursts beat long grinds.
✔ Use the Progress BarThe bar inside each scenario tracks where you are — this reduces overwhelm and builds momentum.
🎓 The Big Picture

Students struggle because they learn the "scientific method" as a list instead of a skill. This tool:

  • States the purpose before you begin
  • Gives a destination — not just a task
  • Explains the long-term payoff (semester → year → two years)
  • Breaks the work into friendly chunks
  • Uses real UWP faculty research as the scenario — making science feel local, real, and achievable
  • Provides clear, immediate feedback to help you refine your answers
