Structure Key
  • 1
    Replication Fork

    The Y-shaped junction where the double helix is unwound and both strands serve as templates for new synthesis.

  • 2
    Leading Strand

    Synthesized continuously in the 5′→3′ direction toward the fork. New nucleotides are added without interruption.

  • 3
    Lagging Strand

    Synthesized discontinuously away from the fork in short Okazaki fragments, also 5′→3′.

  • 4
    5′ End

    The end of a DNA strand that carries a free phosphate group on the 5-carbon of deoxyribose. New strands are built from 5′→3′.

  • 5
    3′ End

    The end carrying a free hydroxyl (–OH) group on the 3-carbon. DNA polymerase adds new nucleotides here.

  • 6
    Phosphate Group (white tube)

    Negatively charged group that links nucleotides via phosphodiester bonds, forming the sugar-phosphate backbone.

  • 7
    Deoxyribose (black pentagon node)

    The 5-carbon sugar of DNA. Its 2′ position lacks an oxygen, distinguishing DNA from RNA.

  • 8
    Nitrogenous Base Pair

    Complementary bases joined by hydrogen bonds: A–T (2 bonds) and G–C (3 bonds), forming the rungs of the ladder.

  • 9
    Parental / Template Strand

    The original double helix that is unwound at the fork. Each strand acts as a template for a new complementary strand.
